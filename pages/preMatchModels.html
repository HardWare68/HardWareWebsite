Model Results

Before starting a Premier game in Counter Strike, you are presented with a screen that has information, such as:

  • Rank of every player
  • What maps the teams have won on recently
  • Any players that are queued together

My goal was to see if, using that information, it would be possible to predict the winner of a match. Here is a plethora of models trained on the data (see the datasets page for the data!), ranked by their accuracy.

Note the two "dummy" models in there, Uniform and Most Frequent. In order to be considered good, a model should do better than these dummy models.

There are two scoring methods as well: accuracy and five-fold score. Accuracy is exactly as it sounds, the model does not see 20% of the data and must predict the result of those matches without having any prior knowledge. Five-fold score comes from using GridSearchCV() to find hyperparameters. Read more about it here. Ideally, accuracy is the best indicator of a model since it has the most insight on how the models perform with no prior knowledge, you know?

You'll also see the training time in seconds it took to to train one fold of the model (recall I am using five-fold validation). A bit of a helpful metric to see how long it takes to train a model, but not very useful as a judge, unless you are worried about time constraints.
